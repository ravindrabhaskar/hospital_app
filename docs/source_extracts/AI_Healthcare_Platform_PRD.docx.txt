1. Product Vision
Create a trusted healthcare companion that helps a person understand what to do next, connects them to the appropriate care pathway, coordinates home and digital care, maintains a longitudinal health record, and supports follow-up and monitoring.
1.1 Product Promise
Understand the user's health concern in natural language or voice.
Guide the user toward an appropriate next step.
Connect the user with qualified healthcare professionals and services.
Keep health information organized in one longitudinal timeline.
Support family caregivers, elderly users and multilingual users.
Use AI with clear safety boundaries and clinician escalation.
2. Problem Statement
Healthcare journeys are fragmented. A patient may have symptoms, old reports, medicines, appointments and multiple providers but still lack a single place that explains what to do next. Teleconsultation solves only one part of the journey; home assessment, records, follow-up, safety monitoring and caregiver coordination often remain disconnected.
Uncertainty about urgency and the right care pathway.
Scattered medical records and incomplete history during consultations.
Difficulty accessing routine care from home.
Manual coordination of doctors, medicines, reports and follow-ups.
Language and health-literacy barriers.
Limited continuity after a consultation.

 | 15. Core User Journeys
15.1 AI Health Journey
User opens Ask AI.
User speaks/types concern.
AI collects essential context.
Safety layer checks defined red flags.
AI provides appropriate next-step guidance or escalates.
User can book care without restarting the journey.
Interaction is stored according to consent.
15.2 Report Upload Journey
User uploads report/photo.
System checks file/image quality.
OCR/extraction is performed where supported.
AI summarizes only supported information.
User sees source report alongside summary.
User can share with a doctor.
15.3 Family Care Journey
Primary user creates/links family member with permission.
Family member profile stores relevant records.
Caregiver receives only permitted alerts.
Appointments and home visits can be managed on behalf of the member.
Access can be revoked.
16. Safety & Clinical Governance
AI must not be positioned as a replacement for a clinician.
Emergency red flags must use deterministic safety rules in addition to generative AI.
High-risk workflows should have human review.
Clinical claims must be validated before production use.
AI outputs require model/version traceability.
Clinicians can override AI-generated suggestions.
Sensitive mental-health safety signals require an appropriate escalation pathway.
Medical image analysis must be validated before any diagnostic claim.
Use representative evaluation datasets across supported languages and relevant populations.

19. MVP Acceptance Criteria
A new patient can complete onboarding and consent.
A patient can ask the AI assistant a health question and receive a safe, structured response.
A patient can search for a doctor and request an appointment.
A patient can request a home checkup and track its status.
A patient can upload a medical report and view it later.
A clinician can view authorized patient history and the home-checkup result.
Critical escalation paths are logged and visible to authorized staff.
Notifications work for core appointment and follow-up events.
Admin can manage users/providers and review audit logs.

 | 21. Risks & Mitigation
Risk
 | Mitigation
 | Incorrect AI output
 | Guardrails, validated knowledge, uncertainty and escalation.
 | Privacy breach
 | Encryption, RBAC, audits, secure infrastructure and testing.
 | Provider quality
 | Credential verification and periodic revalidation.
 | False emergency alerts
 | Confirmation flow and configurable escalation.
 | Missed emergency
 | Conservative safety rules and clear emergency instructions.
 | Low retention
 | Focus on recurring care journeys, not one-off chat.
 | High operational cost
 | Start in a focused geography and use provider partnerships.
 | Regulatory complexity
 | Jurisdiction-specific legal/clinical review before regulated features.
 | 22. Product Design Direction
Primary visual language: white + calm green/mint with restrained accent colors.
Natural human-designed feel; avoid excessive gradients, excessive cards and futuristic clutter.
iPhone 16 Pro Max target reference for premium mobile experience.
Large readable headings and high-contrast primary actions.
Use one dominant CTA per section.
AI should feel approachable and trustworthy rather than robotic.
Accessibility and elderly-friendly interaction patterns should be considered from the beginning.
Bottom navigation: Home, Care, Ask AI, Records, Profile.
23. Final Product Definition
The product is an AI-powered healthcare companion and care coordinator. Its differentiator is the connected journey: understand the concern, determine the appropriate next step, connect the user to home or digital care, organize health information, support clinicians, and continue monitoring and follow-up. The product should launch with a narrow, measurable care workflow and expand only after clinical safety, user demand and unit economics are validated.
