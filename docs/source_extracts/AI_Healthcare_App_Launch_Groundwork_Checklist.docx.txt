Launch GroundworkMaster Checklist
From product concept to controlled pilot and public launch
Document purpose

 | Product
 | AI-powered connected healthcare / care coordination app
 | Primary market
 | India - initial launch in one focused geography
 | Basis
 | Existing Pitch Deck, BRD, PRD, FRD and Complete Product Specification
 | Core launch principle
Do not start by building every feature in the concept. Validate one safe, paid, repeatable care journey first; then expand only after demand, clinical safety, provider delivery and unit economics are proven.

How to use this checklist
Treat each phase as a gate. A phase is not complete because a meeting happened; it is complete when the required evidence or deliverable exists. Where practical, assign a single accountable owner and a deadline for every task.

PHASE 01  |  Freeze the Product Definition
Objective: Decide exactly what the first commercial product is and what it is not.
Recommended starting proposition: AI-powered family healthcare coordination - understand the concern, arrange the appropriate care, maintain the health record and continue follow-up.
 | 
[ ] Write a one-sentence product definition.
[ ] Define the exact healthcare problem being solved.
[ ] Define one primary customer segment.
[ ] Define one secondary customer segment.
[ ] Select one launch geography and service radius.
[ ] Define one flagship care journey to prove first.
[ ] Separate MVP, Phase 2 and future features.
[ ] Define what AI is allowed to do.
[ ] Define what AI is prohibited from doing.
[ ] Define when human or doctor escalation is mandatory.
[ ] Decide whether the business is a marketplace, care coordinator, healthcare provider, SaaS platform, or a defined combination.
[ ] Decide which services will be operated directly and which will be delivered by partners.
[ ] Define the product promise in language a patient can understand in 10 seconds.
[ ] Freeze primary navigation and the major user journeys before design expands further.
Required output
2-3 page Product Definition Document
MVP / Later feature matrix
One flagship care-journey diagram

PHASE 02  |  Customer and Problem Validation
Objective: Prove the problem is painful enough, frequent enough and valuable enough for real users to change behavior or pay.
[ ] Interview 30-50 patients and family caregivers.
[ ] Include at least 10-15 people actively caring for elderly parents or dependents.
[ ] Interview approximately 10 registered doctors.
[ ] Interview nurses and relevant allied-health professionals.
[ ] Interview hospital or clinic administrators.
[ ] Interview home-healthcare operators.
[ ] Interview diagnostic providers.
[ ] Speak with potential employers, insurers or TPAs if B2B2C is part of the plan.
[ ] Document how people currently decide what to do when symptoms begin.
[ ] Document how reports, prescriptions and medication information are currently stored.
[ ] Identify who in the family coordinates appointments, medicines and follow-ups.
[ ] Test willingness to use home healthcare.
[ ] Test willingness to share health information digitally.
[ ] Test AI trust and the conditions that increase or reduce trust.
[ ] Test whether voice and local-language interaction materially improve accessibility.
[ ] Test actual willingness to pay rather than asking only whether users like the idea.
[ ] Rank the top five repeated pain points by frequency and severity.
[ ] Identify the trigger that would make users open this app instead of calling a doctor, relative or hospital.
Required output
Customer interview notes and recordings/consent where applicable
Problem-frequency matrix
Willingness-to-pay evidence
Top three validated user journeys

PHASE 17  |  Go / No-Go Public Launch Gates
Objective: Scale only when demand, care delivery, safety, compliance, technology and economics have all reached minimum acceptable thresholds.
[ ] Customer gate: evidence shows a meaningful, recurring problem.
[ ] Payment gate: target users have paid, not merely expressed interest.
[ ] Retention gate: a meaningful portion of users return or continue their care journey.
[ ] Clinical gate: workflows and SOPs are reviewed and operational.
[ ] AI gate: safety and escalation behavior meets agreed thresholds.
[ ] Provider gate: care can be reliably fulfilled within promised SLAs.
[ ] Operations gate: support, complaints, refunds and escalation processes work under pilot load.
[ ] Legal gate: launch scope and claims have completed regulatory/legal review.
[ ] Privacy gate: consent, access, deletion and audit workflows are implemented.
[ ] Security gate: high-severity vulnerabilities are closed and penetration testing is complete.
[ ] Economic gate: contribution economics are understood and acceptable for the chosen growth stage.
[ ] Technology gate: the critical end-to-end journey works reliably, including degraded/failure modes.
[ ] Support gate: the team can handle incidents and customer escalation.
[ ] Partner gate: third-party services have contractual/SLA coverage appropriate to public promises.
[ ] Executive gate: founders approve a written launch-risk register and rollback plan.
Required output
Signed Launch Readiness Review
Go/No-Go decision log
Launch risk register
Rollback and incident communication plan
