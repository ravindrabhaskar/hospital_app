PART I — Product Thesis & Market Gap
1. Final Product Definition
An AI-powered healthcare companion and care-orchestration platform that helps a patient or family explain a health concern, gathers relevant context, routes the user toward appropriate professional care, coordinates home and digital healthcare, maintains longitudinal health information, and manages follow-up until the care episode is completed.
NOT THE PRODUCT
Do not position the launch product as an AI doctor, autonomous diagnosis system, pharmacy marketplace, generic symptom checker, records locker or doctor directory. Those can be capabilities or integrations; they are not the strategic category.
 | 
2. Core Problem
The problem is care fragmentation. Patients often use separate tools for symptoms, doctor discovery, consultations, labs, medicines, reports and family coordination. The missing value is continuity: what should happen next, who should do it, what information do they need, and did the next step actually happen?
3. Primary Market Gap
The strongest gap is not the absence of individual healthcare features. It is the absence of a consistently integrated, family-aware care journey that begins with an unstructured health concern and stays connected through assessment, professional care, a care plan, records and follow-up.
Gap
 | Current friction
 | Product response
 | Care navigation
 | User must know whether to choose doctor, clinic, test, home care or urgent care.

 | Digital-to-physical bridge

 | Arrange qualified home assessment and feed structured observations to clinician.
 | Care episode continuity
 | Appointments, reports, medicines and follow-ups are disconnected.
 | Make Care Episode the central object linking all events.
 | Family coordination
 | Caregivers manually coordinate parents/dependents.
 | Permission-based family profiles, alerts and care management.

 | Prescription often ends the digital workflow.

 | Doctor reconstructs history manually.

 | Regional-language access
 | Translated UI does not equal understandable care navigation.

 | 
4. Initial Customer Wedge
Primary launch segment: families coordinating recurring healthcare for parents or dependent adults, especially elderly, chronic-care, mobility-limited and post-discharge users. This segment naturally needs care coordination, home care, repeated doctor interactions, records, medication support and family visibility.
5. Market Validation Questions
☐ Will families pay for coordination beyond the cost of underlying services?
☐ Can a reliable home-assessment network deliver within promised service levels?
☐ Will clinicians consistently use a structured pre-consultation summary?
☐ Does AI-first navigation improve conversion to appropriate care without creating unsafe over-reliance?
☐ Can repeat care and family subscriptions produce positive contribution margin?
☐ Can hospitals/clinics use the platform as a post-discharge or between-visit continuity layer?
RULE
Treat these as hypotheses until paid pilots prove them. Do not encode them into investor or marketing claims as established facts.

11. Care Episode Lifecycle
Recommended state model:
NEW → INTAKE → AWAITING_CARE → CARE_SCHEDULED → UNDER_CARE → FOLLOW_UP → RESOLVED
Exceptional states: ESCALATED, EMERGENCY, TRANSFERRED, CANCELLED. Preserve history even when a state changes.
12. AI Architecture
Never use the pattern User → LLM → Medical Answer as the complete clinical workflow. Use a layered architecture:
User Input → Intent/Patient Resolution → Context Retrieval → Structured Intake → Deterministic Safety Rules → Curated Knowledge/RAG → LLM Response/Summary → Policy Check → Service Routing/Escalation → Audit
AI service
 | Responsibility
 | Model gateway
 | Provider/model abstraction, version routing, cost/latency telemetry.
 | Conversation service
 | Natural-language interaction.
 | Structured intake
 | Maps dialogue into typed clinical-intake fields.
 | Context service
 | Retrieves only authorized relevant patient information.
 | Safety engine
 | Clinician-approved deterministic high-risk rules.
 | RAG service
 | Retrieves reviewed source content.
 | Document intelligence
 | Classifies/extracts/summarizes uploaded documents.
 | Clinician summarizer
 | Creates concise source-linked consultation brief.
 | Translation/speech
 | Multilingual STT/TTS and controlled terminology.

 | Model version, prompt/policy version, sources, safety events, test scores.

APPENDIX C — Product North Star
The product is successful when a patient or family can explain a health concern once, the platform safely turns that concern into an appropriate care workflow, qualified professionals receive the context they need, the care plan becomes executable tasks, the family remains appropriately informed, and the episode reaches follow-up or resolution without the user repeatedly reconstructing the journey across disconnected systems.
